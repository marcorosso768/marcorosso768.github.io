I am an applied economist working on <b>political economy</b> and <b>crime economics</b>, and I am an applied microeconomist with a taste for theory working on <b>political economy</b> and <b>crime economics</b>, and I am on the <b><a href="https://www.europeanjobmarketofeconomists.org/candidates?field_candidate_name_target_id=Marco+Rosso&amp;field_phd_granting_institution_target_id=University+of+Bologna">2026–2027 academic job market</a></b>.

My <b>job market paper</b>, <b><a href="https://drive.google.com/file/d/1jNssBLkHiNGftb_r8ujWEBS-c-Jdonwh/view?usp=sharing">The Tipping Point of Temptation: Prevention, Sanctions, and Selection into Public Service</a></b> (<b><a href="https://crenos.unica.it/bibcite/reference/8781">CRENoS WP 26/15</a></b> and <b><a href="https://ssrn.com/abstract=7610318">SSRN</a></b>), asks whether the chance to take a bribe brings more people into public jobs or fewer. A bribe pays today, while the rewards of honesty, and any punishment, come later. In a model built on this timing, a bribe attracts the people who would take it and repels those who would have to keep refusing it. A small bribe can therefore shrink the pool of applicants, and the pool can keep its size while growing less honest. Whether the wage or the number of posts is held fixed, removing the opportunity leaves a corrupt share no larger than a later sanction that cuts the gain from corruption by the same amount.

Having obtained my <b>Ph.D. in Economics</b> at the <b>University of Bologna</b> in 2026, I am a <b>Postdoctoral Researcher</b> at the <b>University of Cagliari</b> and at <b>CRENoS</b>, where I work on remote work, artificial intelligence, and local labor markets: our paper on ChatGPT and posted labor demand (<b><a href="https://crenos.unica.it/bibcite/reference/8766">CRENoS WP 26/13</a></b>) is submitted and was featured by Lightcast. My other <b><a href="/research/">research</a></b> studies voter turnout around childbirth and near public childcare, local crime news and voting, and political selection, drawing also on experimental and behavioral economics.

I <b><a href="/teaching/">teach</a></b> <b>Empirical Methods for Law and Economics</b> in the <b>European Doctorate in Law and Economics (EDLE)</b> and other courses at the <b>University of Bologna</b>, where I also work as a teaching assistant. I also contributed to the <b><a href="https://site.unibo.it/minuto/en">MinUTo Project</a></b> on early child development and to the rollout of the <b><a href="https://appminuto.it/login">MinUTo App</a></b>.
