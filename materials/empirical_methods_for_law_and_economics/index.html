  • Data: Enforcing Contracts, Doing Business 2020 (contracts_2019.dta)
  • source and licence

    Source. World Bank Group, Doing Business, “Enforcing contracts” indicators. Edition: Doing Business 2020 (data as of 1 May 2019); the World Bank’s data files label this year “2019”. Catalogue; downloaded on 6 October 2026 (DB_CSV.zip).

    Licence. Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC BY 4.0). Attribution required. No endorsement by the World Bank is implied.

    Changes made. Selected 8 enforcing-contracts series and 191 economies (economies with a region in the source metadata; regional aggregates excluded; Liechtenstein included although outside the 190-economy ranking); reshaped to one row per economy; renamed and labelled variables; joined region and income-group metadata. No imputation; values unchanged.

    Scope. The indicators describe a standardized hypothetical commercial dispute in the largest business city (two cities averaged for 11 economies), based on expert questionnaires: they are not averages of observed cases. Doing Business was discontinued in September 2021 after data irregularities were found in the 2018 and 2020 editions. Use as historical teaching data. Region and income-group labels are source metadata.

  • Data: GDP and Population, World Development Indicators 2019 (gdp_2019.dta)
  • source and licence

    Source. World Bank, World Development Indicators: GDP (current US$, NY.GDP.MKTP.CD) and total population (SP.POP.TOTL), 2019. Obtained from the normalized CSV mirrors datasets/gdp and datasets/population; downloaded on 13 October 2026.

    Licence. Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC BY 4.0). No endorsement by the World Bank is implied.

    Changes made. Kept 2019; removed regional and income-group aggregates; computed GDP per capita as GDP divided by population; renamed and labelled variables. 210 economies, one row per economy. WDI values are revised over time: these are from the April 2026 vintage.

  • Data: Criminal Justice Personnel and Recorded Offences, Eurostat 2019 (justice_europe_2019.dta)
  • source and licence

    Source. Eurostat, “Personnel in the criminal justice system by sex” (crim_just_job) and “Police-recorded offences by offence category” (crim_off_cat): per hundred thousand inhabitants, both sexes, 2019. Both datasets updated on 29 April 2026; downloaded on 13 October 2026 through the Eurostat API.

    Licence. Free re-use under the Eurostat copyright notice, provided the source is acknowledged and changes are stated. Data for countries outside the EU, EFTA and candidate countries may be subject to restrictions on commercial reuse; this file is for teaching only. The changes below are the instructor’s; Eurostat is not responsible for them.

    Changes made. Selected 2 personnel categories and 6 offence categories; kept 2019; one row per country (37 European countries); merged by ISO3 code with time to enforce contracts and the judicial quality index from Doing Business 2020 (World Bank, CC BY 4.0) and with World Bank GDP per capita for 2019. No imputation; values unchanged.

    Scope. Recorded offences count offences reported to and recorded by the police under national legal definitions: they do not measure how widespread crime is. Professional judges are counted under national definitions. The United Kingdom and Bosnia and Herzegovina have no 2019 values. Kosovo: this designation is without prejudice to positions on status, and is in line with UNSCR 1244/1999 and the ICJ Opinion on the Kosovo declaration of independence.

  • Lectures 5–6 reuse contracts_2019.dta (Week 1) and justice_europe_2019.dta (Week 2).

  • Data: Late Pick-Ups at Day-Care Centres, Gneezy and Rustichini 2000 (daycare_fines.dta)
  • source

    Source. Gneezy, U. and Rustichini, A. (2000). “A Fine Is a Price.” Journal of Legal Studies 29(1): 1–17, Table 1 (“Number of late-coming parents per week according to day-care centers”), p. 6.

    Changes made. The 200 counts (10 day-care centres × 20 weeks) and the number of children per centre were transcribed by the instructor from Table 1 and arranged as one row per centre and week. The variables fine_group and period were added from the study design (Section II.B): the fine was introduced at the start of week 5 in centres 1–6 and removed at the start of week 17. Transcription checked against the period averages in Table 2.

    Notes. As reported by the authors, the study lasted 21 weeks with a one-week holiday after week 14; week 11 had only four days and its counts were multiplied by 5/4 by the authors. Two further centres were dropped by the authors because their records were incomplete. For teaching use; cite the original article.

  • Data: Constitutions of 25 European Countries, One Row per Sentence (constitutions_text.dta)

raw data: how to build the text corpus from the PDFs

  • PDF: Original PDFs, Constitute Project (constitutions_pdf.zip)
  • Text: Text Files, One Sentence per Line (constitutions_txt.zip)
  • Python: From PDF to Text.py
  • source and licence (constitutions)

    Source. Constitute Project: Elkins, Zachary, Tom Ginsburg, and James Melton, Constitute: The World’s Constitutions to Read, Search, and Compare. English versions of 25 European constitutions, downloaded in October 2026. Each PDF states that it “has been generated from excerpts of texts from the repository of the Comparative Constitutions Project, and distributed on constituteproject.org.”

    Licence. Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial 3.0 (CC BY-NC 3.0): re-use and adaptation for non-commercial purposes only, with credit to Constitute Project. The text files and constitutions_text.dta are an adaptation of these texts and are distributed under the same licence. No endorsement by Constitute Project or by the Comparative Constitutions Project is implied.

    Changes made. The PDFs are unchanged. The text files were extracted by the instructor with pdftotext (pdf_to_txt.py): cover page, table of contents, topic index, page headers and footers, topic labels in the margin and headings in larger type removed; words hyphenated at line ends joined; text split into sentences or numbered clauses, one per line (18,853 lines); constitutions_list.csv has one row per constitution. constitutions_text.dta contains the same text as a Stata data set, one row per sentence. No words were translated, corrected or edited; automatic extraction leaves some errors.

    Scope. Most English versions are translations, not the legally binding texts, and some may not include the latest amendments. Italy: the version with amendments through 2012 is used, because the PDF of the 2020 version on Constitute Project contained only the table of contents and the topic index. Switzerland: text provided by the Swiss Federal Chancellery; English is not an official language of the Swiss Confederation and the translation has no legal force. The changes are the instructor’s; Constitute Project is not responsible for them.
